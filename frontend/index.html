Why Softmax Probabilities Fail on Zero-Day Exploits

Conventional deep classifiers force logits through a softmax normalizer: P(y=c|x) = exp(z_c) / Σ exp(z_j). When exposed to an entirely novel Zero-Day exploit with no training precedent, standard softmax often collapses into arbitrarily overconfident predictions due to high magnitude activations in arbitrary dimensions.

Free Energy Function Formulation

ZeroShield solves this using non-probabilistic Gibbs Free Energy:
E(x; T) = -T × log ∑c=1K exp(z_c / T)
Unlike softmax, free energy aligns monotonically with the true data density p(x). Unseen zero-day flows produce low, flat activations, generating high energy scores that immediately cross the calibrated rejection boundary.

FT-Transformer Network Flow Tokenization
Cross-Feature Attention Without Memorization

Most perimeter network models memorize raw IP addresses or ephemeral port numbers, failing immediately when deployed on a different subnet. ZeroShield strictly enforces:

  • No IP Addresses: Purely behavioral and statistical properties.
  • Port Categorization: Raw ports are mapped to standard categories (well-known [0-1023], registered [1024-49151], and ephemeral [49152-65535]).
  • FT-Transformer Token Embedding: Continuous features (packet rates, byte rates, IAT variance, TCP flags) are projected into $d_{model}=64$ vector tokens, allowing self-attention layers to model subtle correlations between flag timing and payload distributions.
